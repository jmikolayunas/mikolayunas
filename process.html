From Data to Heirloom

Every piece begins with a conversation and ends with a legacy. This is the journey from geographic data to handcrafted artwork—where precision meets artistry, and collaboration shapes creation.

01

Discovery

We Begin With Place

Every commission starts with a conversation—not about dimensions or deadlines, but about meaning. What place holds your story? A mountain you climbed with family? The coastline where you scattered ashes? The city where you fell in love?

During the initial consultation, the focus is on listening closely. Your story guides every decision that follows—from the geographic bounds to be captured to the materials that will best honor the place’s character.

Examples at different scales are shared, materials are discussed, and the process of translating topographic data into physical relief is explained. By the end of the call, what’s possible is clear—and the next steps are defined.

  • 30-45 minute video consultation
  • Material samples and references shared
  • Initial feasibility assessment
02

Collaboration

You Shape the Vision

Within 2–3 days of the call, a detailed proposal is delivered along with digital mockups showing how the topography will translate into relief. The proposal outlines the geographic area, vertical exaggeration, orientation, and scale.

This is where collaboration becomes central. Should surrounding terrain be included for context, or should the focus tighten on a specific feature? Cherry with a dark finish, or walnut with a natural oil? Deep blue resin for water, or a subtle translucent pour?

We'll refine the design together until it feels right—not just accurate, but emotionally resonant. Fabrication begins only after the final design is approved; materials are not acquired until that approval is in place.

  • 3D digital mockups with accurate topography
  • Material and finish options presented
  • Detailed timeline and investment breakdown
  • Revisions included until you're satisfied
03

Data Crafting

Science Becomes Art

Once the design is approved and the deposit is received, the technical work begins. High-resolution elevation data is sourced from USGS, NOAA, or international datasets —often combining multiple sources to achieve the best possible detail.

This raw data is processed, cleaned, and translated into toolpaths for CNC (computer numerical control) machining. Vertical exaggeration is tuned so key features read clearly at the chosen scale, and cutting strategies are optimized to preserve fine detail while maintaining structural integrity.

In parallel, the wood is selected. Each board is hand-chosen for grain pattern, color consistency, and figure. Sustainable domestic hardwoods are sourced from certified mills and salvage yards —each piece carrying its own history before becoming part of yours.

  • Geospatial data acquisition and processing
  • 3D model optimization for fabrication
  • Hand-selected wood sourcing
  • CNC toolpath generation
04

Fabrication

Machine Precision, Human Touch

The CNC router carves the topography with sub-millimeter precision over 12–36 hours. The machining process is closely supervised throughout, with feeds and speeds adjusted as needed to prevent tearout and preserve the finest contour detail. What emerges is raw geography—accurate, but not yet art.

Next, the surface is laser-etched with a custom map overlay—trails, tree lines, or waterways —burned directly into the terrain surface with crisp clarity. It’s a quiet layer of storytelling that locks the design to the land before any finish or resin goes down.

Then the hand work begins. Sanding progresses through multiple grits, following the terrain’s natural flow. Edges are chamfered or left raw depending on the aesthetic. Finish is applied—oil, stain, shellac—to enhance grain and add depth-and sealed with a clearcoat.

If the piece includes water features, epoxy resin is mixed and poured in custom colors—built up . Brass inlay is cut and set by hand for summit markers, coastlines, or personal coordinates. Every detail is intentional.

Every frame is built specifically for the piece it surrounds—milled, proportioned, mitered and assembled using spline joinery only. It’s a treaditional, quiet, structural detail that keeps corners crisp and strong without relying on hardware.

The process concludes with laser etching and installation of the stainless steel nameplate, secured with brass hardware. This final detail is fully custom —an inscription that anchors the piece with the place and your story.

  • 12-36 hours of precision CNC carving
  • Up to 16 hours of precision CNC laser etching (if applicable)
  • Hand sanding and finish or resin pour preparation
  • Resin pouring in custom colors (if applicable)
  • Finish application and curing
  • Inlay, gold leaf gilding and detail work
  • Custom framing with spline joinery
  • Nameplate etching and installation
  • Progress photos & videos sent throughout
05

Heirloom

Delivered, Installed, Cherished

The finished piece is professionally photographed and signed on the back with the location details and creation date. A custom shipping crate is built with foam padding and corner bracing—these works ship like the art they are.

Delivery is handled through a specialized art carrier with full insurance and tracking. Local clients may arrange studio pickup, and installation consultation is available —proper mounting is essential for work of this weight and scale.

But delivery isn’t the end of the relationship. Every piece includes care instructions and direct contact for support. Years later, if repair, refinishing, or even a companion piece is needed, the studio remains available. These aren’t products—they’re heirlooms, made to outlive us both.

  • Professional photography and documentation
  • Custom crating and packaging
  • White-glove art carrier shipping
  • Installation guidance provided
  • Lifetime support and restoration services
