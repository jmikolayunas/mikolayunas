From Data to Heirloom

Every piece begins with a conversation and ends as an heirloom.
This is the journey from geographic data to handcrafted artwork—where precision meets artistry, and the work evolves through thoughtful, artist-guided decisions.

01

Discovery

Begin With Place

Every commission starts with a conversation—not about dimensions or deadlines, but about meaning. What place holds your story? A mountain you climbed with family? The coastline where you scattered ashes? The city where you fell in love?

During the initial consultation, the focus is on listening closely. Your story guides every decision that follows—from the geographic bounds to be captured to the materials that will best honor the place’s character.

Examples at different scales are shared, materials are discussed, and the process of translating topographic data into physical relief is explained. By the end of the call, what’s possible is clear—and the next steps are defined.

  • 30–45 minute private consultation
  • Scale, materials, and placement guidance
  • Initial feasibility + next steps
02

Collaboration

You Shape the Vision

Within 2–3 days of the call, a detailed proposal is delivered along with renderings showing how the topography will translate into relief. The proposal outlines the geographic area, vertical exaggeration, orientation, and scale.

This is where collaboration becomes central. Should surrounding terrain be included for context, or should the focus tighten on a specific feature? Cherry with a dark finish, or walnut with a natural oil? Deep blue resin for water, or a subtle translucent pour?

We'll refine the design together until it feels right—not just accurate, but emotionally resonant. I’ll guide the design through clear checkpoints—scale, framing proportion, overlays, and finish direction—so the piece feels inevitable, not forced. Once the core direction is confirmed, fabrication begins, and the work continues to evolve through shared decisions at key moments.

  • 3D renderings with accurate topography
  • Material and finish options presented
  • Detailed timeline and investment breakdown
  • Refinements continue through key checkpoints (mockups, materials, finish, and final detailing)
03

Data Crafting

Science Becomes Art

Once the core direction is set and your build window is reserved, the technical work begins. High-resolution elevation data is sourced from USGS, NOAA, and other international datasets—often combining multiple sources to achieve the best possible detail. The goal is always the most faithful surface the data can support—clean, detailed, and sculptural at the chosen scale.

That raw data is processed, cleaned, and translated into toolpaths for precision carving. Vertical exaggeration is tuned so key features read clearly at scale, and cutting strategies are refined to preserve fine detail while maintaining structural integrity. Along the way, I test options and share results—so decisions are informed by the real surface as it takes shape.

In parallel, the wood is selected. Each board is hand-chosen for grain pattern, color consistency, and figure. Sustainable domestic hardwoods are sourced from certified mills and architectural reclamation yards—each piece carrying its own history before becoming part of yours. Clients are also welcome to provide meaningful material; I’ll evaluate it for stability and suitability, then advise on the best way to incorporate it.

  • Curated terrain data sourcing + preparation
  • Relief modeling + fabrication optimization
  • Hand-selected hardwood selection (client-provided materials welcome)
  • Toolpath strategy + carving preparation
04

Fabrication

Machine Precision, Human Touch

The CNC carving reveals the topography with sub-millimeter fidelity over multiple days (often 12–36 hours of carving time). I monitor the entire cut, adjusting strategy as needed to protect grain and preserve fine contour detail. What emerges is raw geography—accurate, but not yet finished.

Along the way, I test options—finish tone, resin depth, overlay weight—share results, and we choose what feels most true to the place.

Next, the surface is laser-etched with a custom overlay—trails, tree lines, waterways—burned directly into the terrain with crisp clarity. It’s a quiet layer of storytelling that binds the design to the land before any finish or resin is applied.

Then the hand work begins. The surface is sanded through multiple grits, following the terrain’s natural flow. Edges are refined to suit the piece, and the finish is built in layers—oil, stain, or shellac—then sealed for depth and longevity.

Where water is part of the story, resin is poured in custom tones and built up in thin layers for clarity and depth. Brass inlay is cut and set by hand for summit markers, coastlines, or personal coordinates—each detail intentional.

The frame is made specifically for the piece it surrounds—proportioned, milled, and assembled with spline joinery. It’s a traditional, quiet structural detail that keeps corners crisp and strong without visible hardware.

The process concludes with a stainless-steel nameplate, laser-etched and installed with brass hardware —an inscription that anchors the work to place, and to your story.

  • 12-36 hours of CNC carving
  • Laser etching (overlay), when applicable
  • Hand sanding + finish preparation
  • Resin pours (custom tone), when applicable
  • Finish application + curing
  • Inlay + fine detailing
  • Artist-made frame (spline joinery)
  • Custom-engraved nameplate + installation
05

Heirloom

Delivered, Installed, Cherished

The finished piece is professionally photographed and signed on the back with the location details and creation date. A custom shipping crate is built with foam padding and corner bracing—these works ship like the art they are.

Delivery is arranged via insured carrier service with museum-standard crating. Fine-art courier logistics are available by request for large installations or time-sensitive deadlines. Local clients may arrange studio pickup, and installation consultation is available —proper mounting is essential for work of this weight and scale.

But delivery isn’t the end of the relationship. Every piece includes care instructions and direct contact for support. Years later, if repair, refinishing, or even a companion piece is needed, the studio remains available. These aren’t products—they’re heirlooms, made to outlive us both.

  • Professional photography and documentation
  • Custom crating and packaging
  • Specialist art carrier shipping
  • Installation guidance provided
  • Lifetime support and restoration services
