About

Where motion, geography, and material take form.

The work begins with a question of motion. A coastline, a mountain range, a stretch of seafloor may read as fixed, yet each is still becoming. Every landform is mid-process, shaped by forces still active, moving toward a future state on a timescale too slow to observe directly.

Elevation and depth data reconstruct a place's geologic history with precision. Wood, resin, and material judgment hold that history still, briefly, in a single object: evidence of where a place has been, and where it is heading. The resin's translucency keeps the seafloor in view, where the negotiation between land and sea is written.

Each work begins with a place that matters to someone. Research into how that place was formed, its present relationship with the sea or land around it, and the forces still shaping it gives the work its structure. The personal story arrives through the collector, carried by memory and return.

The materials and subjects continue to evolve as the inquiry deepens. Wood, resin, and topography are the current vocabulary for a larger study of motion, geologic time, and the forces that shape the world.

Approach

Still Becoming

A landform is a moment inside an ongoing process. Each piece is built from the same logic that shapes the land itself: water, pressure, resistance, and geologic time made briefly visible in wood and resin.

Place as Identity

We dwell in places until they dwell in us. Geography is where memory lives, and the landscapes that shape us deserve to be held as art, never reduced to souvenirs or screens.

One-of-One

Every piece is made individually, from one place, in an edition of one. Each work is approached with equal care for the geography itself and the process required to honor it. The relationship between work and place is personal and deliberate.
