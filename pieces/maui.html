The Valley Isle

Maui, Hawaii

← Back to Gallery
Materials: koa, birch, resin Dimensions: 29" × 24" × 3" Location: Maui, Hawaii Completed: March 2026

Relief of Maui, an island formed by two shield volcanoes: Haleakalā and Mauna Kahālāwai. Native koa was used for the island's volcanic landform and central valley. Frameless resin surrounds the island in Pacific water, preserving the relationship between land, channel, and open ocean.

Complete topographic composition

Detail of Mauna Kahālāwai

Angled installation view

A close circle of friends commissioned this piece as a wedding gift for a couple who make Maui home. The choice of native koa wood was about honoring what the island holds for all of them: experiences and memories accumulated across visits and years. The material had to come from the place itself, from wood that grows where their collective story lives.

Koa was sourced from a Hawaiian mill committed to sustainable harvest and cultural stewardship. Its tight figure and natural chatoyance bring movement across the carved terrain. Its figure and natural chatoyance catch light differently than mainland hardwoods, creating shifting depth across the carved terrain.

Resin was cast frameless, allowing the island and surrounding Pacific to exist beyond a fixed edge. Bathymetry reveals the terrain below the waterline, connecting volcanic landform, ocean floor, and open water. The open edge was chosen to keep the piece expansive: a place held by friendship, distance, memory, and return. This design choicereflects the nature of the gift itself: a friendship carried across distance, gathered around place, and left room to continue.

— Artist's Note
