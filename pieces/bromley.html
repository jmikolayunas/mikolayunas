Bromley Mountain

Peru, Vermont

← Back to Gallery
Materials: maple and walnut Dimensions: 28" × 17" × 5½" Location: Bromley Mountain, Peru, Vermont Completed: April 2025

Relief of Bromley Mountain, a south-facing Vermont summit carved in maple and walnut. Rounded ridgelines and open slopes give the piece its quieter structure. Laser-etched treelines mark the ski terrain across the southern face.

Shaping of Place

Bromley belongs to the southern Green Mountains, a range shaped by ancient mountain-building, metamorphic rock, long erosion, and glacial movement. Its rounded summit sits below the higher peaks to the north, giving the mountain a broad, approachable profile. Glaciation and weathering softened Bromley’s ridgelines and open slopes, reinforcing the quiet structure that defines the mountain.

The south-facing slope gives Bromley its distinct relationship to light. Sun moves across the open face through the day, making the mountain read clearly from a distance and across seasons. That exposure reinforces the character of the place: broad, steady, familiar, and defined as much by orientation as elevation.

Growing up, Bromley was the client's horizon line, visible from the house through seasons before it ever became a place to ski. The mountain marked weather coming, snow depth, and the promise of winter. Both ways of knowing the mountain guided the work: first as landmark, then as terrain.

The carving was shaped around Bromley’s softer profile. Rounded ridgelines, open slopes, and the south-facing aspect guided the relief toward a form familiar at a glance.

The framing was expanded beyond the ski trail network, with more room held around the mountain’s full form. The treelines and trail openings were etched with a more organic character, set within the broad open sweep of Bromley’s terrain.
